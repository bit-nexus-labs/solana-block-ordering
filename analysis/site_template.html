Before the leader's window, the fee decides. Inside it, arrival does.

We sent our own transactions, with random fees in a random send order, into the leader windows of mainnet validators and read the blocks back. No client follows one strict rule. Which rule wins depends on when the batch reaches the leader, and that was confirmed by a pre-registered test.

When exactly does the fee decide?

  
Exploratory, not pre-registered. The fee wins clearly only when our transactions both wait together and lock the same account; independent transactions are not sorted by fee even when they wait. Arrival order has a time resolution: inside the window, transactions sent more than ~30 ms apart keep their send order in 90–95% of cases. The fee acts as a rank, not as a bid size: one fee level above the neighbour already wins 75% of such pairs, five levels 81%. And a batch that waits for the window lands at the top of the block: over direct QUIC, 91% in the leader's first slot, half of it in the first 11% of the block.

  






  
The path decides whether timing matters

  
Direct unstaked QUIC loses more than half of each batch, but what arrives, arrives when we chose. A public RPC delivers more, yet forwards to several leaders on its own schedule, and the timing effect disappears.
